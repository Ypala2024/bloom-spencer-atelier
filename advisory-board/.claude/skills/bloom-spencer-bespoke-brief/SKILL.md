# Bloom & Spencer Bespoke Brief

Bloom & Spencer is a luxury jeweller in Begumpet, Hyderabad, known for lab-grown diamonds and coloured gemstones. Clients often start with a short, messy message ("looking for an engagement ring, she likes green, budget around 2 lakh"). Today a staff member re-reads it, asks the same questions, explains the same stone facts, and writes the brief for the workshop by hand. This skill does that the same way every time.

## Goal
From one enquiry, produce three things a staff member can use straight away:
1. A **client reply** that makes the client feel looked after and moves them toward a consultation.
2. A **design brief** for the design/workshop team.
3. A short list of **questions still open** before a quote can be given.

## Inputs
- The enquiry text, call notes or a screenshot (any language mix: English, Hindi, Telugu, Hinglish).
- Optional: the client's name, previous purchases, reference photos.

## Step 1: Extract what the client said
Fill only what is stated or clearly implied. Mark everything else "Not given". Never guess a budget, ring size or date.
- Occasion (engagement, wedding, anniversary, gift, self-purchase, festival)
- Piece: ring / studs / pendant / bracelet / other
- Stone(s) and colour wishes
- Cut / shape
- Metal: yellow gold, white gold, rose gold, platinum
- Style words (classic, minimal, bold, vintage, halo, solitaire, three-stone)
- Size details (ring size, chain length, wrist)
- Budget (keep the client's own words and currency, e.g. "around ₹2 lakh")
- Deadline / event date
- Wearer's lifestyle (daily wear, hands-on job, occasional wear)

## Step 2: Recommend (use these facts, don't invent others)
| Stone | Mohs hardness | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Diamond (lab-grown at B&S; chemically and optically the same as mined) | 10 | Any piece, daily-wear rings |
| Ruby (corundum) | 9 | Rings and daily wear; romantic, bold |
| Sapphire (corundum) | 9 | Engagement rings; classic, heritage |
| Emerald (beryl, natural inclusions or "jardin") | 7.5-8 | Pendants, studs, halo or bezel-protected rings; not ideal for hands-on daily wear |
| Paraiba tourmaline (copper-coloured, very rare) | 7-7.5 | Earrings and pendants; statement pieces |

Cuts offered: Round, Oval, Pear, Marquise, Princess, Emerald cut. Metals: yellow, white and rose gold, platinum.

Rules of thumb:
- Softer stones (emerald, Paraiba) in a ring for daily wear: suggest a protective setting (halo or bezel), or a pendant or studs instead. Say why, kindly.
- Pointed shapes (pear, marquise, princess) need protected tips (V-prongs).
- Oval and pear look larger for the same carat; useful when the budget is tight.
- White gold or platinum makes diamonds and sapphires look icier; yellow gold warms emeralds and rubies.
- Give **one main recommendation and at most one alternative**, each with a one-line reason.

## Step 3: Write the client reply
Voice: quiet luxury. Warm, unhurried, never pushy.
- Use the client's name if known. Mirror their language lightly (a Hinglish message can get one warm Hindi phrase; keep the rest in English).
- 60-120 words for WhatsApp. No emoji walls (one at most).
- Acknowledge the occasion, give the recommendation in one or two sentences, ask at most **two** of the open questions, and invite them to a consultation (in the Begumpet studio or by video call).
- **Never** mention discounts, offers, "limited time", "buy now" or urgency. Never quote a price; say pricing is confirmed at consultation.

## Step 4: Write the design brief (for staff)
```
BESPOKE BRIEF: <client name or "New enquiry"> · <date>
Occasion:        ...
Piece:           ...
Stone / cut:     ...  (recommended) | alt: ...
Metal:           ...
Setting/style:   ...
Sizes:           ...
Budget:          <client's words>
Needed by:       ...
Lifestyle notes: ...
Design notes:    <2-4 bullets: proportions, protection, inspiration from the collection>
Open questions:  <numbered>
Next step:       Book consultation / send reference images / await sizing
```

## Step 5: Self-check before handing back
- Every field in the brief traces to the enquiry or is marked "Not given".
- Hardness numbers match the table above.
- The reply contains no price, discount or pressure language.
- At most two questions in the reply; the rest stay in the brief.

## Guardrails
- Don't send anything to the client yourself. A staff member reviews and sends.
- Don't promise delivery dates, certification grades or prices. Those come from the store.
- Don't store or repeat client phone numbers or addresses in the brief beyond what staff need.
- If the enquiry isn't about jewellery (a complaint, a repair, an order status), say so and draft a polite hand-off to staff instead.

## Output order
1. Client reply (ready to paste)
2. Design brief
3. Open questions

## Example
Input: "hi, want to propose next month, she loves green and wears rings daily. budget ~2L, gold"

The skill recommends an emerald in a yellow-gold halo (the halo protects a 7.5-8 hardness stone for daily wear), with a green-toned alternative such as a pear lab-grown diamond with emerald side stones. It asks for ring size and the proposal date, and invites the client to a consultation. The brief marks the budget "~₹2 lakh (client's words)" and the deadline "next month (exact date not given)".
